What Affects the Cost of Building a Mobile App in Pakistan?

By the Kamboh Technologies team4 November 20266 min read

“How much will my app cost?” is the first question almost every business asks, and the honest answer is: it depends on what the app needs to do. Two apps that look similar on the surface can take very different amounts of work behind the scenes. This guide explains the main factors so you can plan a realistic budget and compare quotes fairly.

1. The number and complexity of features

Features are the biggest driver of cost. A simple information app with a few screens is very different from an app with user accounts, payments, live tracking and a chat system. Each feature needs design, development, testing and ongoing maintenance.

A useful exercise is to list every feature and mark it as must-have or nice-to-have. Launching with only the must-haves (often called an MVP, or minimum viable product) gets you to market faster and lets real users tell you what to build next.

2. One platform or two

Your customers probably use both iPhone and Android. Building two separate native apps means two codebases to build and maintain. A cross-platform framework such as Flutter lets one codebase run on both, which usually reduces build time and long-term maintenance without giving up a native look and feel.

3. Design and user experience

A polished, easy-to-use design is not decoration; it decides whether people keep using your app. Custom design, animations and a clickable prototype take time, but they also prevent expensive changes later because you test the idea before development starts.

4. The backend and admin panel

Most apps need more than the app itself. Behind the scenes there is usually:

  • A database and API that store users, orders and content
  • An admin panel where your team manages everything
  • Notifications, file storage and security

Using a managed backend service can speed this up, while a custom backend gives more control. Either way, it is a real part of the project and should be in the quote.

5. Integrations with other services

Payment gateways, maps, SMS, courier tracking or your existing ERP each add work. Every integration needs to be connected, tested and handled gracefully when the other service is slow or unavailable.

6. Testing, launch and app store approval

Testing on different devices and screen sizes, fixing issues and preparing App Store and Google Play listings all take time. Apple in particular reviews every app, so store preparation should be part of the plan rather than an afterthought.

7. Maintenance after launch

An app is never truly “finished”. New iOS and Android versions, security updates, bug fixes and new features all need ongoing work. When comparing quotes, ask what support after launch looks like.

How to get an accurate quote

The more clearly you describe your app, the more accurate the estimate. Before contacting any development team, prepare:

  1. A short description of the app and who it is for
  2. The three to five things users must be able to do
  3. Any apps you like as references
  4. Integrations you need, such as payments or maps
  5. Your ideal launch timeframe

A good team will then ask questions, suggest what to include in the first version and give you a written scope and estimate, so you know exactly what you are paying for.

The bottom line

The cost of an app comes down to scope. Start with a focused first version, choose a cross-platform approach where it makes sense, and plan for maintenance from day one. That is how you launch sooner, spend wisely and grow the app based on real feedback.
